# Harries Vogue: voice and copy rules

Read this before writing any words for the site, emails or social. Based on
`assets/HowtowritecopiesthatSoundLikeYou.md`: decide first, then write. Sell the outcome, not the fabric.

## The three decisions

1. **Who we're talking to.** A Lagos woman, roughly 25 to 50, with an occasion coming: her wedding,
   a friend's wedding where she's in the aso-ebi, a burial, a naming, an owambe. She has a tailor and
   a date. She has been let down before: the fabric "finished" after she told the group, the colour
   was different in person, or she lost a Saturday in market traffic. Often she buys for others too.
2. **The one thing she takes away.** *You'll walk in on the day looking the way you pictured it, and
   getting the fabric there was the easy part.*
3. **Why she should believe us.** A real shop in Abule Egba, Lagos. Every bolt shows exactly how many
   yards are left. Her yards are held for 15 minutes while she pays. Paystack takes the payment; we
   never see her card. (Add proof as it becomes real: reviews, photos of real customers, years trading.
   Never invent it.)

**The one-liner:** Harries Vogue gets you dressed for the day you'll remember: the right fabric, the
exact yards, no running around.

## Outcome first

The homepage sells the day, not the shop: how she looks, how she feels, what people say, the photos she
keeps. How buying works (yards, stock, holds, Paystack) belongs only where she's buying: the price list,
the yard slip, the product page, checkout and the questions. Every section answers "what does this do
for her?" before "what is it?".

The table below is for those buying moments, not for homepage headlines.

| Feature (what it is) | Outcome (what she gets) |
| --- | --- |
| Live stock per bolt | No "it has finished" after you've told the aso-ebi group |
| Yard picker, 1 to 30 | Buy what your tailor asked for, see the total before you commit |
| 15-minute hold | Nobody takes your yards while you're paying |
| Paystack | Your card stays private |
| Lingerie + ready-to-wear | One order covers the outfit, what goes under it and the day after |
| Shop in Abule Egba | Feel it in your hands first if you want to |

## Tone: three words

- **Warm, like the woman at the counter.** She knows the occasion matters. Never cutesy, never "babe".
  *"Your tailor says six yards. You buy six yards."*
- **Sure.** Short sentences. No hedging, no exclamation marks, no superlatives we can't check.
  *"When it says three yards left, there are three yards left."*
- **Lagos-real.** Name the real things: owambe, aso-ebi, gele, the tailor, the aunties, the photos,
  market traffic. *"The aunties in matching gele."*

## Rules

- Specific beats grand. "Exact yards, held 15 minutes" persuades; "premium quality" doesn't.
- Write it the way you'd say it across the counter, then cut it in half.
- Banned words: elevate, curated, solutions, luxury (show it instead), premium, passionate, world-class, unleash.
- Never invent proof (reviews, customer counts, press) or policies (delivery days, returns). Use `[TBC: …]`.
- Clarity where she's deciding (prices, buttons, checkout, policies). Warmth in the margins (headlines,
  empty states, emails, the confirmation page).
